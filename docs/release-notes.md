Tacit keeps memory for coding agents in Markdown and git. Use a local store or share one across machines.

- **Not everything you say becomes a rule.** A ruling that rests on words you said in one session is now your statement: your words verbatim, the day, the session and a line of context, shown as "you said once". Only the same point said again in a separate session, or a check that agrees, makes it a decision or a rule again (the statements step's ladder). Code counts the sessions. A resumed, forked or imported copy of a session counts once, and a ruling you wrote in the app stays yours. The curator does this for new notes. `tacit rulings one-offs` does it for a store curated before, with no model call. Each ruling it changes points to its statement and is never deleted.
- **The reviewer trims instead of asking.** Most notes the reviewer was unsure of are a true core plus one overreach. Now it says what is in doubt, the core or only an overreach. For an overreach it cuts the note down to the claims its session supports, each citing the messages that support it. It lists the claims it removed, each with why. Code checks that the cut only removes or narrows: every claim cites a message the reviewer was shown, no new claim, no "the owner said" that no message of yours says, and the scope only narrows. The trimmed note is curated in the note's place as a trusted agent note, labelled "trimmed to what its session supports". The note stays in the inbox, marked replaced, with its removed claims and their reasons in the ledger. When what is kept rests on one remark of yours, the result is your statement, never a rule.
- **Questions only where they matter.** A question is queued only when the core of a rule, a decision or a past mistake is in doubt, in a project whose record says active, and your words in the store or a check do not already settle it. At most 12 wait, and that cap now holds when a question is queued. The rest stay quiet, low-weight agent notes, and the rotation checks them first.
- **Not every sentence of yours is a statement.** Wherever your words would become a statement or a rule (the dreamer, the curator, the reviewer's trim, `tacit rulings one-offs`), Opus also judges whether the remark matters to future work: an instruction, a decision, a preference, a fact about a project, a machine or the world, or a correction is kept; an acknowledgement ("ok", "go", "yes do it"), a passing question, frustration or one-time steering ("run it again", "now push") is dropped. Code keeps a remark only with one of those categories and a reason, and still checks the words are yours. A dropped remark is not curated. It stays in the inbox note or the session it came from, with the reason in the run's report or the ledger. `tacit rulings one-offs` now runs this gate (one Opus call per 20 rulings), writes the kept ones as your statements and retires the dropped ones. `--dry-run` reports both counts and a sample, and `--verdicts FILE` reuses a dry run's judgements, so the write costs nothing more.
- **The reviewer knows what it did not read.** In a long session the reviewer's digest leaves the oldest messages out and cuts long ones short. The reviewer is now told how many and where. Each removed claim says why: unsupported, contradicted, widens, or gives you authority you did not give. For a claim removed as unsupported, code searches what the reviewer was not shown for the claim's distinctive words (never the note's own save), and the reviewer reads what it finds and judges the claim again. A claim is never removed as unsupported while code finds its words there: the trim is refused, and the note stays as it was.
- **`tacit reviewer --retrim`** reads again, for their trim, the notes the reviewer was unsure of before this release.

Measured on a copy of the rig's rebuild store (2026-10-25, read-only from the rig):
- **Rulings.** 1,133 of its 1,139 live rulings rest on words said in one session. 6 rest on two sessions or more. Counted without merging copies, 68 seemed to: the rest were Codex forks and Hermes continuations of one conversation.
- **The 127 uncertain notes, read again ($39.67 on Opus 5.5).** 10 were judged correct the second time; the earlier verdict stands. 47 were trimmed: 23 to a trusted note, and 24 to your statement (16 of these with a trimmed note beside it for the agent's own claims). Code refused 20 trims, most often a rule kept on your words without your remark. Those notes stay uncertain. For 50 the core itself is in doubt.
- **Questions.** None were queued on the rebuild store, which has no project records yet. With the rig's live store's records, 12 were queued (the cap), 12 more were held back by the cap, 12 were settled by your words already in the store, and 13 were not about an active project.
- **The relevance gate on the one-offs (2026-10-26, the rebuild store at b8277790, $3.96 on Opus 5.5).** 1,187 of 1,216 live rulings rest on words said in one session. The gate kept 933 as your statements (389 decisions, 308 instructions, 88 preferences, 80 facts, 68 corrections) and dropped 254 (233 one-time steering, 8 questions, 5 acknowledgements, 5 frustration, 3 other). None were left unjudged.
- **The 7 trims that had removed a claim sitting outside the digest, read again ($2.19).** Two were judged correct the second time (the earlier verdict stands). In 4 of the 7 the second look searched what the reviewer had not been shown. One claim was put back on the second look, citing the message code found. In three notes a claim was still called unsupported while code found its words, so those trims were refused and the notes stay uncertain. In one, both claims were removed as contradicted by later messages, which the hand check agreed with. Three of the seven digests were not over budget at all. There the missing support was in tool results, such as your answers to an agent's multiple-choice question or a side agent's report, which the reviewer does not read yet.
- **15 trims checked against their whole sessions.** 6 were right, 8 had the right core with one flaw, and 1 was wrong. Kept claims held in 14 of 15. The common flaw was removing a claim as "not in the conversation" when it sits in the part the reviewer's 160,000-character digest leaves out. Nothing is lost: the note and its removed claims stay. Two of the 8 statements were a default you set on purpose and confirmed in the same session.

Upgrading: no index change (search index schema 15), store format 4. The reviewer's ledger gains the verdicts `replaced` and `trimmed`, and its lines may carry `relevance`, `omitted`, `looked` and each removed claim's `basis`. `review/statements.jsonl` gains `dropped` lines, which an older binary passes over. The dreamer's prompt is v8. A binary older than this one skips those lines, so until every writer runs this version an older one may curate a replaced note as before. After upgrading the curator's machine, run `tacit reviewer --retrim`, then `tacit rulings one-offs --dry-run --json FILE` to see what would change (about $4 for the rebuild store), then the same without `--dry-run` and with `--verdicts FILE`, then `tacit statements`.

Builds for Linux, macOS and Windows on amd64 and arm64 include the CLI, the browser app and a compatible scrump redactor. Git 2.29+ is required. [Setup and platform validation](https://github.com/agent-sh/tacit/blob/main/docs/portable.md).
